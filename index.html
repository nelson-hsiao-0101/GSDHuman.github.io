GSDHuman reconstructs photorealistic, dynamic human avatars from a single monocular video — in minutes.

Abstract

Fast surface reconstruction from one camera

Reconstructing animatable human avatars usually demands multi-camera rigs, studios, or motion-capture suits. GSDHuman reconstructs dynamic avatars from an ordinary monocular video.

3D Gaussian Splatting renders efficiently but struggles to recover precise surface geometry on articulated bodies. GSDHuman instead uses 2D Gaussian surfels — oriented planar disks that stay view-consistent — anchored to a SMPL body prior and deformed with Linear Blend Skinning. Geometry-aware pruning and a Wasserstein-distance densification keep the surfel field clean, and a dedicated opacity loss encourages faithful, extractable geometry.

The result is a fast, high-fidelity path from footage to a textured mesh, validated on People-Snapshot and in-the-wild video. GSDHuman is Module 1; the companion system 3DFilmPCA adds real-time pose and shape editing.

Method

Surfels on a body prior, kept clean

GSDHuman initializes 2D Gaussian surfels on the SMPL template in canonical space, deforms them into the posed space with Linear Blend Skinning, and regularizes the distribution so the surface stays clean and extractable.

Mesh extraction. RGB, surface normals, and depth are fused (TSDF) and meshed with marching cubes into a clean, geometry-consistent model.

01

Geometry-aware pruning

Outlier pruning removes surfels far from the body; normal-consistency pruning drops splats whose orientation disagrees with their neighbors — so what remains conforms to the surface.

02

Wasserstein densification

A surfel densifies only when its Wasserstein distance to its nearest neighbor exceeds a threshold, avoiding the redundant clusters that gradient-only strategies pile into dense regions.

03

Opacity regularization

A dedicated loss pushes surfels toward fully opaque or transparent. An empirically best weight of 0.005 yields clean, connected meshes; larger weights collapse training.

04

Mesh extraction

Rendered RGB, depth, and normals are fused in a TSDF volume (Open3D VoxelBlockGrid) and meshed with marching cubes, keeping the largest connected component.
